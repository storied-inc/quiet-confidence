# Contributing

This repo is a standard, not a codebase, so what's useful here is different from a normal pull request.

## What helps most

**Before-and-after pairs from your own sent mail.** The field guide teaches through contrast, and the examples get better the more of them come from real exchanges rather than invented ones. Anonymize the counterparty to a role. Keep the "before" as something a capable person would actually send, never a strawman.

**A named defect we haven't named.** Unrequested rationale is in the guide because it earned a name: a specific, recurring move with a clear cost. If you can name another one that precisely, open an issue with two examples.

**A place the register breaks.** Contexts where the guidance is wrong, cultures where restraint reads as cold, roles where peer footing isn't available. Those are more useful than agreement.

## What to skip

Reworded principles, added principles that restate an existing one, and expansions. The guide is short on purpose. Authority by subtraction applies to the document too, so a pull request that adds length has to retire something to pay for it.

## How to open something

Issues are the front door. A short issue beats a large pull request, because the discussion about whether a change belongs is usually the whole work.

If you're adapting this for your own firm, you don't need permission beyond the credit the license asks for, and you don't need to contribute anything back. Reword it in your own voice and install it wherever you keep your standards. That's the intended use.
